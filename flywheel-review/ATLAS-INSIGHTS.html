X3D Flywheel Atlas: from a magnificent fan to a working learning engine

Prepared from the supplied x3d-flywheel-work-2026-11-17.zip snapshot. Archive labels and source pins are retained as provenance; this is an assessment of those bytes, not a refreshed inventory of GitHub. The distinction matters because a repository, renderer, instrument, and claim can each advance on a different clock.

Delivered in this iteration

The accompanying prototype implements 12 feathers: Ontology, ML, Maths, STEM, Gameplay, Game development, Physics, Music, Data intensity, Heritage, Blender IO hub, and Architecture. The larger domain table later in this document is a proposed expansion, not a claim of additional implemented adapters.

The new gobbler-flyte/index.html displays the recount of the actual archive, offers SHA-256-bound Atlas search, and lets a visitor select each proposed domain interface. Its four-stage synthetic foldback walkthrough is protocol-checked, with a separate positive and negative control required for every guard and a distinct consuming domain. It is a teaching reconstruction of fm/1, law/7, and the control discipline of law/44; it does not execute a shader experiment. The original cinematic remains at cinematic.html.

A separate executed shader guard now performs one narrow foldback on five newly authored fixtures. It rejects raw GLSL mistaken for a URL, accepts the repaired scene and ordinary shader URL controls, and passes a distinct music-labelled consumer through the same check. foldback-run.json binds its code, source records, and fixtures by digest. This is a real local structural run; shader compilation, visual output, and live cross-repository adoption remain unproved.

The new portable audit passes 9 of 9 local consistency checks. Fifty-two regression tests across the kernel and audit cover tampered hashes, missing digest coverage, bad pins, duplicate identities, unresolved references, missing controls, controls pooled across different guards, and repeated reuse. New scene geometry is rebuilt deterministically and checked for XML, DEF/USE/ROUTE consistency, matching data digests, and interface completeness. Browser rendering could not be verified in this session: the available browser could not reach the local preview server. XSD validation, hardware GPU timing, phone rendering, and audio listening remain unperformed for this iteration.

The central finding

There is already a credible foundation: X3D supplies an inspectable spatial representation, the Loom supplies disciplined construction and failure records, and the Atlas supplies the beginnings of a machine-queryable memory. Gobbler supplies an unusually expressive visual language for the whole undertaking. The next advance is to make the links between those four things executable.

The unit of progress should be a reusable, evidence-bound improvement. A new scene is an output. A scene that exposes a defect, creates a regression fixture, changes a check, and prevents the defect in a second domain has completed a flywheel turn. Repository activity, graph size, and the speed of a rendered wheel can help explain work; none measures that turn on its own.

Gobbler is best understood as an agent coordinator with a visible memory. Its feathers are capability interfaces; its wheels are repeated experiment loops; its loom binds artifacts to evidence; its Atlas remembers the relationships. The feather fan can be expansive and fantastical while every claim in its witness panel remains concrete.

Three evidence levels must remain visibly distinct:

  1. Locally checked structure: counts, hashes, JSON endpoints, XML references, and query execution checked against the uploaded bytes.
  2. Carried historical result: the original receipt records RDF equivalence, transcription checks, validator results, and earlier browser inspection. It names the tools and pins used then.
  3. Proposal or illustration: domain assignments, future integrations, artistic pulses, simulated workload, ranking weights, and desired operating behavior.

The most dangerous ambiguity is the word “verified.” The Atlas's own 1,360 copied-row claims were recorded as verified transcription: copied values matched quoted source material at pinned locations. This is different from the 84 domain Claim nodes, different again from rerunning the experiment behind a number, and different from a human judging a world beautiful or useful. Do not combine them into a single trust score.

Reproducibility is the immediate engineering gap

The complete Atlas builder cannot presently be replayed from this archive alone. build_atlas.py loads learn_lib.py and claims_lib.py from flywheel-review/src/x3d.docs/docs/tools-shared/, a tree absent from the ZIP. check_atlas.py also needs rdflib; it is not installed in the available Python environments. The original validator helper and witness refer to an absolute the original author's home-directory x3d_mcp path (now X3D_MCP_DIR) checkout.

The correct response is to expose the limitation and repair packaging. A missing dependency must never silently fall back to a stale green receipt. A future portable checker should emit one result per check: pass, fail, or not_run, with reason, tool version, and input digest. Keep the original receipt intact and write the new run separately.

The report in FLYWHEEL.md also carries GitHub activity, visibility, licensing, and adoption observations. Those observations are historical source statements. This assessment does not promote them into current facts. Repository permissions, rights, and publishing decisions should be checked at the intended target before a release.

A sharper architecture: artifacts, runs, claims, decisions, transfers

The current Atlas is strongest as a ledger index. A working flywheel needs a second, connected model of activity.

Record Essential fields What it answers
Artifact stable ID, media type, content digest, source locator, license identifier or unresolved state What exact thing did we make or inspect?
Run artifact IDs, tool and engine pins, environment, parameters, start/end or explicit timeless build, exit status, output digests What exact procedure happened?
Observation run ID, observable, value, unit, uncertainty or tolerance, instrument, evidence attachment What was actually measured?
Claim proposition, scope, supporting/refuting observations, derivation, status, reviewer What are we entitled to say?
Decision candidate IDs, criterion, selected option, reviewer, rationale, alternatives, superseded decision Why did we change direction?
Transfer source failure/claim, target fixture/check, consuming domain, confirming run What learning crossed a boundary?

Add first-class supersedes, refutes, invalidates, dependsOn, and appliesWithin edges. A fresh renderer version should invalidate a claim's applicability when appropriate without deleting the historical run. A contrary observation should remain inspectable beside the earlier one. A graph that only accumulates agreement becomes an archive of confident repetition.

A minimal event envelope can be implemented with Python's standard library before any graph database is introduced:

{
  "schema": "flywheel.event/0.1",
  "event_id": "event/example-001",
  "kind": "regression_confirmed",
  "domain": "blender_io_hub",
  "artifact": {"id": "artifact/example", "sha256": "<64 hex digits>"},
  "run": "run/example",
  "claim": "claim/example",
  "derived_from": ["failure/example", "fixture/example"],
  "scope": {"engine": "<pin>", "platform": "<pin>"},
  "evidence_state": "reproduced",
  "synthetic": false
}

The envelope is a proposal, and the placeholder values are intentional. A schema-valid event is still an assertion until its references, hashes, procedure, and result are checked. Content-addressing detects mismatched bytes; signatures and independently trusted anchors address who vouched for them. Neither proves physical truth.

The smallest complete turn

Use one scene, one defect, two consuming domains:

  1. Author a small X3D scene containing a deliberate attribute or animation defect.
  2. Validate and render it under pinned tools; store both pass/fail outputs and scope.
  3. Store the minimal failing scene and a corresponding known-good scene.
  4. Add a check that rejects the failure and accepts the good control.
  5. Export through Blender or a bridge; show the same check catches the defect there.
  6. Add the failure, check, fixture, run, and transfer to Atlas.
  7. Inspect the resulting world and record one human usability judgment.

Completion is a connected chain with a successful negative control. Animation in the demonstration should make this chain comprehensible. Only a real run should add a real completed turn.

A practical first instance is an X3D material/geometry identity invariant through Blender export. It can connect authoring, ontology, source identity, rendering, and teaching without introducing another physics engine or training a model. Choose a narrowly specified invariant that the available exporter actually preserves; preserve unsupported cases as explicit failures.

Gobbler as a usable agent abstraction

The metaphor works when each part corresponds to an operational affordance:

Visual element Meaning Interaction
Heart Current task, selected question, or integration goal Inspect success criteria and budget
Feather Domain capability and its current contract Select fixtures, dependencies, evidence, and next experiment
Flywheel A repeatable loop that retains learning Open the latest run and see which stage blocked
Warp threads Stable IDs, units, schemas, invariants Inspect the contracts shared across domains
Weft threads Particular experiments and artifact transfers Replay the connected run chain
Gold knot An observation bound to a pinned source or run Read the witness and its scope
Broken or amber thread Missing, stale, disputed, or unsupported dependency Open a repairable work item
Cast feather A superseded hypothesis or retired adapter Follow the replacement; history remains visible

Use speed and brightness carefully. A wheel can turn as ambient art, but explain that speed is an artistic parameter. In an instrumented mode, let one discrete pulse represent one accepted event. Do not derive a “confidence” percentage from the number of graph edges, and do not let a hundred dependent claims look like a hundred independent witnesses.

Recommended presentation modes:

  • Wonder: spacious, cinematic, low text density; the fan, wheels, and threads invite exploration.
  • Investigate: selected feather, actual graph facts, stable IDs, scope, and “how do we know?” controls.
  • Experiment: modify one parameter, make a prediction, run a bounded procedure, compare results.
  • Review: open failures, conflicting evidence, unjudged visual/audio outputs, and proposed transfers.

A curious beginner should be able to select Physics, see a pendulum, vary one quantity, and discover the relevant law. An advanced visitor should be able to inspect the timestep, integrator, tolerance, renderer pin, graph path, and exact evidence without leaving the same conceptual world. Progressive disclosure is the bridge between those audiences.

Where compounding actually comes from

Accumulation creates value only when it changes later behavior. The Atlas can do more than answer queries:

  1. Before authoring: retrieve a small set of applicable laws, counterexamples, known tool limits, and fixtures. Log which items were retrieved.
  2. During construction: provide typed contracts and targeted checks, naming their actual coverage.
  3. After a failure: reduce it to a minimal case, distinguish source defect from adapter defect, and attach it to a failure class.
  4. Before accepting a repair: run both the minimal case and a known-good control, then test a second relevant consumer.
  5. During reuse: record whether the returned knowledge helped, was irrelevant, or caused a false warning.

This closes the missing edge from knowledge to changed action. Search relevance by itself does not establish that a recommendation prevented a defect.

Use a vector of measures instead of a universal momentum score:

Measure Definition Main trap
Trace coverage In-scope claims with resolvable evidence / in-scope claims Confusing quotation with reproduced measurement
Reproduction coverage In-scope claims with a passing current run / in-scope claims Moving the scope to hide difficult claims
Regression escape rate Confirmed known defect instances missed / tested known defect instances Using only easy mutants
Transfer yield Transfers confirmed in another consumer / proposed transfers Renaming the same consumer as another domain
Recurrence Repeated instances of already-covered failure classes in a fixed window More testing can reveal more instances without worse engineering
Review debt Outputs awaiting the appropriate human judgment, with age Agent review substituted for cultural or aesthetic authority
Retrieval utility Accepted useful recommendations / reviewed recommendations, by task Self-rating by the recommending agent
Cost of a confirmed improvement Compute, time, review effort per accepted repair/transfer Ignoring verification and maintenance costs

The important experiment is causal: hold tasks and scorer fixed, compare authoring with and without Atlas retrieval, preserve unsuccessful attempts, and inspect the difference. The initial sample can be small and exploratory; report uncertainty and avoid presenting a pilot as proof of general model improvement.

A bounded advancement sequence

PR 1 — Make the snapshot portable and honest

Change: supply or reproducibly fetch pinned builder dependencies; replace machine-specific paths with explicit configuration; add a snapshot-only checker that does not require source checkout; retain historical receipt separately from current runs.

Acceptance: a fresh machine can validate snapshot hashes, endpoints, counts, and XML references offline; full-source reproduction either completes from declared dependencies or reports a precise not_run; a corrupted file produces failure. No stale receipt is overwritten by a partial run.

PR 2 — Bind Gobbler's feathers to Atlas

Change: define feather contracts and curated memberships in data; show actual Atlas facts and stable-ID examples when a feather is selected; add explicit artistic/illustrated states for domains without sufficient indexed evidence. Keep the existing art available as a preserved baseline.

Acceptance: each displayed artifact-derived count has a deterministic query and digest; every selected example resolves to an Atlas ID; the scene and panel share domain IDs; malformed input fails visibly; ambient motion cannot be mistaken for real event throughput.

PR 3 — Complete one real transfer

Change: implement the deliberately broken scene → witness → repair → check → second consumer chain described above. Ingest its artifacts and runs into the activity model.

Acceptance: the planted defect fails before repair, passes after repair, a good control stays good, and an independent consuming path demonstrates the transfer. The graph can answer which incident created the check and which later run benefited.

PR 4 — Make memory act on the next task

Change: expose bounded, read-only Atlas queries through a small service or MCP adapter; add retrieval logging and a miniature held-out authoring evaluation. Start with the existing Python query interface; no graph database is necessary for 2,181 nodes.

Acceptance: malformed/oversized requests are bounded, results include stable IDs and source pins, repeated requests are deterministic for the same graph digest, retrieval failures are explicit, and the comparison preserves both successful and failed authoring attempts.

PR 5 — Close the human witness loop

Change: a small queue for scene, sound, and learning-experience review, beginning with one actual reviewer and one concrete question per item.

Acceptance: blind comparisons preserve candidate identity internally, selections record criterion and context, unreviewed outputs remain visible, and agent-generated preferences are never substituted for recorded human judgment.

Defer a model fine-tune, a planet-scale graph deployment, extra engine bridges, and full autonomous nightly promotion until these five steps have meaningful evidence. The domains can keep expanding conceptually while the operational core remains small enough to understand.

Immediate commands and review questions

The snapshot query CLI is already useful:

python3 atlas/atlas.py stats
python3 atlas/atlas.py laws-for ShaderPart ROUTE --json
python3 atlas/atlas.py what-broke 'white|blank' --json
python3 atlas/atlas.py how-do-we-know N-TRC-1 --json
python3 atlas/atlas.py stands-on 'X_ITE 16.6.0' --json
python3 atlas/atlas.py worklist --json

Run these from flywheel-review/. The full check_atlas.py --offline path additionally requires the missing pinned source tree and dependencies described above. A successful CLI query demonstrates access to stored knowledge, not renewed validation of the knowledge.

For each proposed feather or enhancement, ask:

  1. What can a person do here that they could not do before?
  2. What exact artifact, observation, or decision will survive the session?
  3. What falsifiable check could reject our claim of improvement?
  4. Which second domain can reuse the result without trusting the author?
  5. What does the visual language reveal about uncertainty, provenance, and remaining work?

The most promising destination is an inspectable experimental commons: a place where an expressive spatial world, rigorous construction tools, learning experiences, and a growing memory reinforce each other. The grand fan earns its scale one traceable, transferable improvement at a time.
